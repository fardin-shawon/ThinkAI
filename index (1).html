Core Principles

How to Think
About AI

Before you type a single prompt, understand these foundations. They're what separates people who get shallow outputs from those who get real, useful results.

01 🎯

Be the Pilot, Not the Passenger

AI is powerful but directionless without you. You set the goal, define the context, and judge the result. Never hand over your critical thinking — stay in control of every step.

02 🔍

Verify Before You Trust

AI can hallucinate facts, dates, and sources with complete confidence. Treat AI output like a first draft from a smart intern — impressive but always needs checking before it leaves your desk.

03 🔐

Guard Your Privacy

Never share passwords, personal ID numbers, medical records, or company secrets with AI tools. Even well-intentioned systems can expose data. Keep sensitive information off the table.

Prompt Library

Prompts That
Actually Work

📚 Learning

Learn Any Concept Deeply

Get a structured breakdown of any topic with clear analogies. Great for students or self-learners tackling something new.

Try this →
Explain [topic] to me like I'm a curious 16-year-old. Start with the core idea in one sentence, then give me three real-world examples, and end with why it matters today.
💡 Thinking

Challenge Your Own Ideas

Use AI to steelman or stress-test your arguments. This is critical thinking at its best — don't let AI agree with everything you say.

Try this →
I believe [your opinion]. Play devil's advocate — give me the three strongest arguments against this view, and tell me which one you think is hardest to counter.
✍️ Writing

Sharpen Your Writing

Instead of asking AI to write for you, use it as an editor. You keep your voice; AI helps you communicate it better.

Try this →
Here's something I wrote: [paste text]. Don't rewrite it. Instead, point out: 1) where I'm unclear, 2) where I repeat myself, 3) my strongest sentence. Keep my voice.
💼 Work

Prepare for a Difficult Meeting

Use AI as a thinking partner before high-stakes conversations. Walk in prepared for every angle.

Try this →
I have a meeting about [topic] with [who]. My goal is [outcome]. What are the likely objections or concerns they'll raise? How should I respond to each one?
📚 Learning

Build a Study Plan

Stop aimlessly studying. Get a structured, time-bound plan tailored to your actual goal and starting point.

Try this →
I want to learn [skill] in [timeframe]. I'm a complete beginner. Create a weekly study plan with specific resources and daily tasks. Be realistic about what I can achieve.
💡 Thinking

Make a Better Decision

Structured decision-making with AI as a sounding board. Surfaces things you might have missed while keeping you in the driver's seat.

Try this →
I'm deciding between [option A] and [option B]. My priorities are [list]. What am I probably not considering? Don't tell me which to choose — help me think more clearly.
💼 Work

Summarize Long Content

Feed AI a report, article, or document and extract exactly what you need — without losing the nuances that matter.

Try this →
[Paste document]. Summarize this in three parts: 1) The main argument in one sentence, 2) The three most important facts or findings, 3) What action this implies I should take.
✍️ Writing

Write a Cold Email That Gets Replies

Cold outreach is hard. Use this prompt to draft something genuine, specific, and human — not a generic blast.

Try this →
Write a cold email to [person/role] at [company]. My goal is [specific ask]. I want it to feel personal and show I've done research. Keep it under 120 words and avoid all clichés.
💡 Thinking

Fact-Check an AI Answer

Never trust AI blindly. This meta-prompt makes AI tell you exactly where it might be wrong — and what to check.

Try this →
You just told me [paste previous AI answer]. Now: what in that answer are you least confident about? What should I independently verify? Are there any claims that might be outdated?
Common Pitfalls

Mistakes People
Make With AI

01

Vague, One-Line Prompts

Typing "write me an essay" and expecting a masterpiece. AI needs context — who's it for, what's the goal, what tone, how long?

Give AI a role, context, constraints, and a clear goal in every prompt.
02

Accepting the First Draft

The first AI response is rarely the best one. Great results come from iterating — asking follow-up questions and pushing further.

Say "That's close — now make it more concise" or "Give me a bolder version."
03

Not Verifying AI "Facts"

AI sounds authoritative even when it's wrong. It can cite fake papers, invent statistics, and misquote real people — all confidently.

For anything important, cross-check with a reliable source. Never use AI as your only reference.
04

Sharing Private Information

Pasting passwords, customer data, or confidential business strategies into public AI tools is a serious security risk.

Anonymize or remove sensitive data before using AI tools. Use enterprise versions for sensitive work.
05

Outsourcing Your Thinking

Using AI to think for you instead of with you. Your judgment, experience, and ethical reasoning aren't replaceable.

Use AI to explore and expand ideas — then apply your own judgment to decide and act.
06

Ignoring Bias in Outputs

AI reflects patterns in its training data, which means it can reproduce biases around culture, gender, perspective, and more.

Ask AI to consider multiple perspectives. Be especially critical when outputs feel "too agreeable."
